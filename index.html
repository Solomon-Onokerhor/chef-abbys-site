(02) — The Journey

From site plans
to supper plans.

Eight scenes from a Kumasi kitchen to the world stage. Scroll slowly. Some of it is still simmering.

1999
Scene 01

A Kumasi girl
and a pot.

Abena Amoakoaa Sintim-Aboagye grew up in Kumasi, in the heart of Ashanti. Wesley Girls' Senior High, then KNUST to study Construction Technology and Management. The plan was buildings. The pull was always the kitchen.

Scene 02

The lockdown
pot.

Stuck at home during the pandemic, she filmed what she cooked. First on Snapchat, then TikTok and YouTube. No studio and no crew, just a phone, a pot and Ghana's classics.

“I love to teach people how to cook, so I made it a profession.”
Scene 03

Food becomes
a language.

Waakye, kenkey, jollof and banku became short films about who eats them and why. She took the camera to the markets and taught visitors, Michael Dapaah among them, to wrap kenkey by hand. In 2024 she was nominated for Food Creator of the Year at the Creator Awards.

Scene 04

Accra
to Cannes.

The only African creator in TikTok's delegation to Cannes Lions, with a private dinner with CEO Shou Zi Chew. She used her seat to ask how African creators get into the global conversation. A month later, TIME named her to its first TIME100 Creators list.

“Storytelling has no borders.”

Scene 05

A menu
for a King.

Official culinary partner for King Charles III's birthday reception in Accra. She worked with the Residence's chef on a Ghana and Britain fusion menu. She also hosted London mayor Sadiq Khan for a private dinner on his visit to Ghana.

Scene 06

Ghana
on the road.

It started in January with IShowSpeed, live from Ghana, introducing him to kosua ne meko. Then a 25-day, 12-region tour for Heritage Month, an appointment with the Office of Diaspora Affairs and, in May, a UK food tour cooking with London's food creators.

Scene 07

Listen. Learn.
Connect. Share.

The West Africa Food Tour began in August 2026: Togo, then Benin's markets, then Senegal. Home kitchens, street food and indigenous ingredients, filmed to get Africans travelling Africa through its food.

“I'm here to listen, learn, connect, and share.”

16 October · World Food Day

Accra & Kumasi

Scene 08

The whole family
packs the boxes.

The first Big Street Feast in 2025 fed Accra's streets for free, three meals across the day for more than 15,000 people. For the second, she aimed for 50,000 across two cities and asked everyone to pitch in. On the eve of the feast she posted a video of her 78-year-old father in the kitchen, quietly packing food boxes.

“When you cook a country's traditional food, you learn its values, its struggles, and its soul.”

(07) — On the menu

The classics.

Five dishes Ghana grew up on, and the ones she keeps cooking for the world.

01 / 05

  1. 01 · One-pot rice · Everywhere

    Jollof

    Rice cooked down in tomato, pepper and onion until every grain is red and a little smoky at the bottom of the pot. West Africa argues about it. Ghana is certain.

    Her pickThe meal she comes home to after every trip.

    Eat it withFried chickenShitoSalad

  2. 02 · Rice & beans · Northern roots

    Waakye

    Rice and beans cooked with dried sorghum leaves, which give it that deep wine colour. Bought in the morning, wrapped in a leaf, and piled with everything the seller has.

    Her pick“Everyone will love it.” The dish she recommends first.

    Eat it withShitoGariSpaghettiEggWele

  3. 03 · Fermented corn · The coast

    Kenkey

    Fermented corn dough wrapped in husks and boiled until firm and sour. Ga kenkey in corn husk, Fante kenkey in plantain leaf. The one she taught Michael Dapaah to wrap.

    Eat it withFried fishFresh pepperShito

  4. 04 · Corn & cassava · Volta & Accra

    Banku

    Fermented corn and cassava dough stirred over the fire until smooth and stretchy, then shaped by hand. Eaten with your fingers, always.

    Eat it withOkro stewGrilled tilapiaPepper

  5. 05 · Street food · After dark

    Kelewele

    Ripe plantain cut into cubes, tossed in ginger, chilli and spice, and fried until the edges catch. Sold hot from roadside pans once the sun goes down.

    Eat it withRoasted groundnuts
